Breakup of liquid metal filaments

A numerical simulations of metal filaments on a solid substrate. Liquefied by a laser, the filaments breakup governed by the Rayleigh-Plateau instability. The Marangoni effect, resulting from a different surface tension of the two metals from which the filament is built, can lead to the inversion of the breakup process, producing droplets at the locations where according to the Rayleigh-Plateau theory dry spots would be expected.

These simulations are created with Gerris fluid dynamics solver and The Persistence of Vision ray tracing software as part of Galery of Fluid Motion competition at the Physics of Fluids conference.

This work was done in collaborations with Kyle Mahady, Shahriar Afkhami & Lou Kondic at New Jersey Institute of Technology.
